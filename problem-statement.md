Problem statement
Every year, millions of people relocate to cities they have never lived in — for college, jobs, or a fresh start. When they search for a place to live, they face a fragmented, exhausting research process: one tab for crime statistics, another for Google Maps, another for Reddit threads, another for walk scores, another for rent comparisons. There is no single platform that pulls all of this together. The result is that people make one of the most important decisions of their lives — where to live — based on incomplete, scattered, and often outdated information. First-time movers, out-of-state students, and long-distance relocators are especially vulnerable, because they have no local knowledge, no friends to ask, and no way to feel the neighborhood before signing a lease.

What NeighborIQ does
NeighborIQ is an AI-powered neighborhood research platform that turns any US address into a 7-point compatibility score in under 10 seconds. A user types an address, selects their lifestyle profile — student, family, remote worker, young professional — and instantly receives a structured breakdown across seven categories: crime safety, nearby amenities, natural disaster risk, transit and walkability, cost of living, nightlife and social scene, and community sentiment. Each category is powered by a live data source — SpotCrime, Google Places, FEMA's National Risk Index, Walk Score, RentCast, Yelp, and the Claude API with Reddit web search — so every score reflects real, current information rather than cached averages. The lifestyle profile dynamically reweights the categories so the same neighborhood can score differently for a student than it does for a family, making the result feel genuinely personal rather than generic.
What makes NeighborIQ different from anything that exists today is the community sentiment layer — an AI model that reads hundreds of real Reddit posts about the neighborhood and synthesizes them into a plain-English summary and a vibe score. No Zillow, no Apartments.com, no Walk Score does this. Beyond the score itself, users can compare up to three addresses side by side, ask follow-up questions through a Claude-powered neighborhood chatbot pre-loaded with all the data already fetched, and share a clean report link with roommates or family. NeighborIQ is not just a scorecard — it is the local friend you call before signing a lease, available for any address in America.
